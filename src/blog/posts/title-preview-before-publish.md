The title that fits the editor is not the title viewers see. Search results, the home feed and profile pages all truncate text at different points, and once it is cut, the rest is invisible. A preview is the cheapest way to catch that.

## How YouTube cuts a title

A search card shows roughly two lines of the title - often 60 to 70 characters, depending on the letters and the screen. Narrow letters fit more, wide capitals fit fewer, and the cut lands mid-word. Whatever sits after the cut does not exist for that viewer.

That has a practical consequence: the hook and the topic belong in the first few words. "I tried 30 cameras - here is the one I kept" survives a cut. "A long story about testing thirty cameras and the model that stayed" loses its point.

## How Instagram cuts a caption

The feed shows about the first two lines before a "more" link. The first line carries the hook; everything after the cut is one tap away and mostly unread. Emoji at the start can eat width without adding meaning.

## Different text, same check

The trick is to stop writing a "best case" version for the editor and a truncated version for the platform. Write the short version first, then let the preview show how the full text behaves on each card. If the important part survives on the smallest crop, the longer platform versions can be built on top.

## Running the check

The [Title & Caption Preview](/title-preview/) takes a title, a channel name and a caption and draws them as a YouTube search card and an Instagram post, clamping at the real cut. Adjust the wording and watch the cut move. Nothing is saved or sent, so half-finished titles are fine to test.

## A short routine before publishing

- Put the payoff word or number inside the first 50 characters.
- Avoid brackets and repeat words that push the topic past the cut.
- Write the caption's first line as a standalone sentence.
- Check the preview at phone width, where most of the audience reads.

Typing the title once and checking it in three places takes a minute. Fixing a misleading truncated title after a video has traffic takes much longer.
