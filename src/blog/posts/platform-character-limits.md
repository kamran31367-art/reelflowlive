Every platform has a hard limit on titles and captions, and they are all different. A caption that fits TikTok can be cut off on X, and a YouTube title that looks fine in the editor can lose its ending in search results. Knowing the numbers turns that guessing into a single check.

## The numbers

- **YouTube title:** 100 characters
- **YouTube description:** 5,000 characters
- **TikTok caption:** 2,200 characters
- **Instagram caption:** 2,200 characters
- **X post:** 280 characters

These are the limits the platforms enforce on save. Visible limits are shorter: a YouTube title is truncated in search well before 100, and an Instagram caption collapses to the first line or two behind a "more" link.

## How emoji and hashtags count

Counting characters by bytes or by code units gives wrong answers for emoji. A family emoji or a flag is a single visible character but many underlying units. The [Character Counter](/character-counter/) counts whole characters - grapheme clusters - so what you see is what the platform counts.

Hashtags are part of the caption and count like any other text. The tool also builds a list of unique hashtags, which is useful when a caption has been edited several times and the same tag appears twice.

## One caption, four platforms

The practical routine:

1. Write the full caption for the platform with the most room (TikTok or Instagram).
2. Put the strongest sentence first. On every platform, the opening is what people see before any truncation.
3. Write a short version for X - 280 characters - by keeping the hook and dropping the rest.
4. Keep the YouTube title under about 60 characters so it survives search truncation even though the field allows 100.

This is also how hashtags stay sane: pick five or six that matter, put them at the end, and do not pad the list to look busy.

## The count that actually matters

A caption at 2,199 characters technically fits. It also means the useful part is buried under paragraphs nobody reads. The limit is a ceiling, not a target. For most short videos, 150-300 characters of clear text outperform a wall of hashtags.

For descriptions with links and credits, the 5,000-character YouTube limit is the one to watch - chapter lists, affiliate blocks and timestamps add up faster than expected. Check the count before pasting the final version.

## Before you publish

- Count the final text, including every hashtag and emoji.
- Keep the first line self-contained; assume everything after it may be hidden.
- Shorten for X rather than pasting the long version and hoping.
- Keep the YouTube title under 60 characters even though the field takes 100.
