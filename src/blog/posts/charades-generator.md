Charades needs very little equipment, but it does need prompts that suit the room. The [Charades Generator](/charades-generator/) produces a ready-to-use list from five categories: movies, animals, actions, objects and food. It is handy when you want to start a party or classroom game without building a deck first.

The tool handles the random selection. You still handle the timer, turn order and scoring, which keeps it useful across different age groups and game formats.

## What the generator is designed to do

A good charades prompt is something a performer can communicate without speaking or writing. The built-in lists cover familiar titles, animals, physical actions, everyday objects and food-related tasks.

Different categories create different rounds:

- **Actions** often work well for younger players and movement-focused games.
- **Animals** and **Objects** suit quick single-concept guesses.
- **Food** mixes object recognition with tasks such as making or eating something.
- **Movies** rely more on shared cultural knowledge.

**All** combines every category. That variety can be entertaining, but a mixed batch may be uneven, so a host should preview it rather than assuming every prompt will land with every group.

## Controls and available prompts

The page has two main controls:

- **Category:** All, Movies, Animals, Actions, Objects or Food.
- **How many:** a displayed range of 1 to 100, starting at 12.

Press **Generate prompts** to replace the result. There is no difficulty selector, uniqueness toggle or custom text area.

Each category contains 12 built-in prompts. Examples include *Titanic* and *Star Wars* in Movies, penguin and giraffe in Animals, brushing teeth and playing guitar in Actions, umbrella and alarm clock in Objects, and peeling a banana and making pizza in Food.

The combined pool therefore contains 60 prompts before repetition. Use the displayed count range rather than relying on unusually large typed values; the operation itself does not clamp the number input.

## Worked example: six action prompts

Choose **Actions** and set **How many** to 6. One possible result is:

```text
brushing teeth
playing guitar
walking a dog
typing on a laptop
watering plants
dancing
```

The actual order and selection will change each time. A category batch can also repeat an item, so treat this as an illustration of the format, not a promised six-card set.

These prompts are suitable for a quick warm-up round because each describes a visible task. For a quieter or accessibility-aware session, agree in advance that seated gestures and hand-only acting are valid alternatives to full-body movement.

## Output and file formats

Results appear as plain text, one prompt per line. Capitalisation in film titles and object names follows the built-in list; other prompts are generally lower-case actions or objects.

**Copy** copies the complete batch to your clipboard. **Download** saves a UTF-8 text file named `charades-generator.txt`. That file can be printed, edited for personal use or kept with other game notes.

There is no PDF card export, slideshow mode, random-prompt reveal button, audio cue or scoring display. If you need individual cards, split the downloaded lines yourself before the game.

## Repetition and category limits

The generator shuffles the selected pool and keeps drawing from it until it reaches the requested count. Items are not removed after selection, so duplicates are possible.

That matters most when the count approaches or exceeds the category size. Asking for 20 Actions prompts draws from only 12 distinct entries, making repeats unavoidable. Even a smaller request can repeat because there is no uniqueness check.

There is also no semantic difficulty ranking. Movies may be unfamiliar to younger players, while a multi-step action can be harder to communicate than a short animal name. Preview the list and select categories deliberately rather than treating every batch as equally accessible.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not show the full list to the guessing team. A shared screen containing future prompts gives away answers before the performer starts.

Other avoidable problems include:

- Choosing All without checking whether film titles suit the group.
- Expecting a difficulty filter that does not exist.
- Requesting more prompts than the category can supply uniquely.
- Using speaking, mouthing or writing when the group agreed on silent acting.
- Forgetting to decide what happens when a performer gets stuck.

Keep the rules simple and consistent. For example, allow one pass per turn, award a point for a correct guess before time expires and stop the round at a fixed number of prompts.

## A practical party or classroom workflow

1. Choose a category that matches the players' knowledge and mobility.
2. Generate a small batch, usually one more prompt than you expect to use.
3. Review the text for repeats and unsuitable titles or actions.
4. Copy it to a private host view or download it for offline use.
5. Set a timer and agree on pass and scoring rules.
6. Show or read only the current prompt, then hide it after the guess.

For a classroom, Actions and Animals are easy starting categories. You can also use one prompt as a vocabulary exercise: after the guess, ask the class to name the verb or object in a sentence. That extension belongs to the activity, not the generator.

For remote play, one participant can act while another privately holds the current prompt. Avoid sharing the complete batch in the call chat unless future prompts are meant to be public.

## Browser privacy and local processing

The selected category and count are processed locally in the browser. No player names or performance data are requested by the generator, and no custom prompt text is uploaded.

The main privacy risk is the place you send the output. A clipboard may be read by other apps under your device's permissions, and an online game document may be visible to collaborators. Keep the host list private when future prompts are meant to remain secret.

## Final checklist

- The category suits the players' age, knowledge and mobility.
- The count matches the planned turns.
- The batch has been checked for repeats and unsuitable prompts.
- Only the current prompt is visible to guessers.
- Timer, pass and scoring rules are agreed.
- The host has a private copy or downloaded file.

With those checks complete, the list is ready. The generator's job ends at the prompt; a good round depends on clear turns and a timer everyone can see.
