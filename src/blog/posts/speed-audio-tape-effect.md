There are two different ways to change the speed of audio. One keeps the pitch and squeezes time; the other changes both at once, like spinning a record faster. This tool does the second one, and knowing which you are using decides whether the result sounds right.

## The tape effect

Play a tape faster and the voice rises and the file gets shorter. Play it slower and the voice deepens and the file grows. Speed and pitch move together because nothing is separating them - that is the honest description of this mode.

The [Audio Speed & Pitch tool](/audio-speed-pitch/) exposes both controls, with the pitch slider stacked on top of the speed. Every adjustment changes the length of the export.

## When the tape effect is the point

- **Meme and comedic voices** - the chipmunk or slowed-and-reverbed sound is a genre of its own.
- **A quick pitch check** - drop a vocal an octave to hear the melody, or raise eight bars to find a harmony.
- **Nightcore-style edits** where speed and pitch rising together is the style.

## When it is the wrong tool

- **Fitting a voiceover into a fixed slot.** If a 34-second read must fit 30 seconds and still sound like the same person, this tool will make it sound like someone else. Time-stretch is the right tool, and this page does not claim to do it.
- **Transcription prep.** A faster file is harder to transcribe; keep the original.
- **Music tempo matching** that must preserve key.

If the pitch shift is unwanted, undo it on a copy - repeated tape-style passes compound, and each pass loses a little quality.

## The waveform image

The same page draws the loudness of the loaded audio as a clean PNG. For a video thumbnail, podcast cover or show notes, a waveform strip is a recognisable visual and says more about audio content than a generic microphone graphic. Save it, drop it into the design, and use a section of it rather than the whole thing when space is tight.

## Before exporting

Listen to the start, the middle and the end - speed changes sometimes clip a fade. Export is WAV, so the result is uncompressed; convert afterwards if a smaller file is needed for messaging.
